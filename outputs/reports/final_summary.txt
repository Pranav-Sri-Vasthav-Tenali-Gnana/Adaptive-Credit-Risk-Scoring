naive_recent = most recent year only; sliding = 3-year window (deployed);
expanding = all history. Finding: ALL retraining beats static, and RECENCY
beats VOLUME - naive_recent edges out the sliding window, while the expanding
window is the weakest adaptive strategy by 2018 (0.7154 < 0.7222 < 0.7297),
i.e. keeping stale 2015-16 data actively hurts once drift sets in. The sliding
window is the robust compromise: it bounds memory without needing the optimal
lookback to be known in advance, and tracks naive_recent closely.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Demonstrated real concept drift in Lending Club credit data (2015-2018)
     with THREE independent detectors that agree: the default rate rose 20.5%
     -> 27.2% (KS label drift), PSI and Jensen-Shannon distance flagged

  3. Showed drift-triggered adaptive retraining (sliding window) recovers
     performance on both AUC and the threshold-free PR-AUC, with the gain

     2018.
  4. Benchmarked retraining STRATEGIES (naive most-recent-year, 3-year sliding
     window, full expanding window) against a naive-baseline reviewer concern;
     found that RECENCY beats VOLUME - the expanding window is the weakest
     adaptive option by 2018 (stale data hurts), naive-recent is strongest, and
     the deployed sliding window is the robust compromise. Also clarified the
     drift TRIGGER's value is operational (skipping unnecessary retrains) since
     drift fires every year on this data.
  5. Built an end-to-end, MLflow-tracked MLOps loop coupling the multi-method
     drift detection to automated retraining triggers, with validation-tuned
     decision thresholds (avoiding the misleading fixed-0.5 F1 artifact) - a

  6. Added SHAP-based explainability-stability monitoring (cosine, Kendall tau,

6. LIMITATIONS & THREATS TO VALIDITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Data ends in 2018: the Lending Club public release contains no loans
     issued 2019-2023, so the COVID-era shock - the most extreme recent credit
     drift - cannot be tested; the observed drift is the milder 2015-2018
     trend.
  2. Single dataset / single market (US unsecured consumer loans), so the
     magnitude of degradation and recovery may not transfer to other portfolios
     or geographies.
  3. Yearly windows are coarse: real monitoring would operate monthly; annual
     granularity smooths over intra-year drift and limits the number of drift
     events.
  4. Labels require loan maturity (only resolved Fully Paid / Charged Off loans
     are used), so the most recent originations are under-represented - a
     survivorship/label-latency caveat inherent to credit outcomes.
  5. SMOTE is applied to balance training; while thresholds are tuned on a
     held-out real-prevalence validation split, probability calibration (e.g.
     isotonic) was not performed and would be needed before using scores as
     probabilities.
  6. Drift fires every year on this data, so the trigger cannot here be shown
     to REDUCE retraining frequency versus a periodic schedule - only to avoid
     retraining when a single detector (PSI) is quiet; a longer, lower-drift
     series is needed to show that.
